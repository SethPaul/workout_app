# Training Evidence Brief
Rules for auditing a workout pool for a recreational garage-gym athlete (~4 d/wk, CrossFit-style: barbell, olympic lifts, kettlebell, rower/bike, bodyweight). Goals: strength, power, stamina.

## 1. Strength
**R1.** Main-lift reps/load: 1–6RM (70–100%1RM); 8–12RM acceptable for strength-hypertrophy overlap. Advanced lifters periodize 1–12 reps, emphasizing 1–6RM. *Conf: high.* — ACSM Position Stand, Med Sci Sports Exerc 2009;41(3):687-708 (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579); update: Med Sci Sports Exerc 2026, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416.

**R2.** Sets/muscle group/wk: minimum-effective ≈4–6, upper-useful ≈10, ceiling ~12–20 for advanced. Each added weekly set ≈+0.37% hypertrophy (p=0.002); threshold ~10 sets/wk for near-max hypertrophy; medium(4-9)/high(≥10) sets beat low(<4) for strength in trained lifters. *Conf: high (trend), moderate (exact counts).* — Schoenfeld/Ogborn/Krieger, J Sports Sci 2017;35(11):1073-82 (pubmed 27433992); Ralston et al., Sports Med 2017;47:2585-2601 (PMC5684266).

**R3.** Rest between heavy sets (≥80%1RM): 2–3+ min; 90s–2min for accessory. Longer rest gives modest strength benefit (SMD≈-0.74) vs. trivial hypertrophy benefit (SMD≈0.08); RCT: 3-min beat 1-min rest for strength+thigh hypertrophy. *Conf: high (strength), low/contested (hypertrophy).* — Schoenfeld et al., J Strength Cond Res 2016;30(7):1805-12; medRxiv 2025 meta-analysis; Frontiers 2024 Bayesian review (PMC11349676).

**R4.** Frequency/lift: ≥2×/wk as a safe floor. Higher frequency correlates with more strength gain, but effect disappears when volume is equated — frequency is a volume-delivery vehicle, not independently causal. *Conf: moderate.* — Grgic et al., Sports Med 2018;48(5):1207-20 (pubmed 29470825).

## 2. Power / Olympic Lifts
**R5.** Reps/set 1–3 (classic lifts), 3–5 (pulls/derivatives); load 70–90%1RM main work, briefly ≥85–90% to peak. Prilepin's chart: 70–80%→3-6 sets×3 (18-30 total lifts); 80-90%→2-4 sets×2-3 (10-20 total); >90%→1-2 reps, 4-10 total. *Conf: moderate (coaching-log derived, reaffirmed by NSCA).* — NSCA Position Statement on Weightlifting (Comfort/Haff/Suchomel et al.), J Strength Cond Res 2023;37(6):1163-90.

**R6.** Rest for oly-lift sets: 2–5 min; never to failure — bar speed/technique degrade fast under fatigue, and <30%1RM loads are also unstable (poor deceleration control). *Conf: high (technique consensus).* — NSCA Weightlifting Position Statement 2023.

**R7.** Oly lifts/derivatives go first in a session (after warm-up, before strength/conditioning): they need peak neural precision/bar speed, both degraded by prior fatigue. *Conf: high.* — ACSM 2009; NSCA 2023.

**R8.** High-rep oly lifts under fatigue (5–10+ reps for time/EMOM) are discouraged: technique breakdown under fatigue is a recognized injury mechanism; drop load sharply (≤60-70%1RM) as reps/fatigue rise. *Conf: moderate — mechanistically strong, epidemiologically thin (evidence gap).* — NSCA 2023.

**R9.** EMOM clean/snatch: appropriate at 1–3 reps/interval, 50–75%1RM, with rest built into the minute; not as max-rep/AMRAP couplets at high load. *Conf: moderate*, follows from R6–R8.

## 3. Accessory / Hypertrophy-Adjacent
**R10.** Reps 8–15+ (occasionally 15-30); proximity to failure 0-3 RIR is sufficient — training to absolute failure shows no extra hypertrophy/strength vs. stopping ~1-2 reps short, and non-failure trended better for strength. *Conf: moderate-high.* — Grgic et al., J Sport Health Sci 2021 (pubmed 33497853).

**R11.** Rest 60–90s for accessory/isolation work; long-rest strength benefit (R3) is smaller for single-joint, submaximal work. *Conf: moderate.*

## 4. Stamina / Conditioning
**R12.** Tabata: 20s work/10s rest ×8 (4min) at supramaximal intensity (~170%VO2max, cycle ergometer) → +14% VO2max, +28% anaerobic capacity in 6wk, 4-5d/wk. Result is intensity/modality-specific; doesn't generalize to sub-maximal "20/10 circuits." *Conf: high (original protocol), low (generalized use).* — Tabata et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 1996;28(10):1327-30.

**R13.** Norwegian 4×4: 4×4min @90-95%HRmax, 3min active recovery, 2-3×/wk → large VO2max gains (58.1→64.3 mL/kg/min in 8wk) without reported strength interference. *Conf: high.* — Helgerud et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2007;39(4):665-71.

**R14.** Billat 30/30: 30s @vVO2max/30s easy, repeat to failure — accumulates 15-20+min near VO2max via O2-uptake lag; lower orthopedic cost than long intervals. *Conf: moderate.* — Billat et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 1999;31(1):156-63 (pubmed 9927024).

**R15.** True high-intensity/VO2max-intensity minutes: ~20-40min/wk (2-3 sessions of 15-20min). Remaining conditioning volume: low intensity ("Zone 2," <~75-80%HRmax). Polarized distribution (~75-80% low/~15-20% high/~0-5% moderate) outperforms pyramidal/threshold-heavy for VO2peak. *Conf: moderate — evidence from endurance specialists; extrapolation to mixed-modal athletes is reasoned, not directly tested.* — Silva Oliveira/Boppre/Fonseca, Sports Med 2024 (PMC11329428).

**R16.** AMRAP/for-time WODs are a distinct mixed stimulus — legitimate for work-capacity but shouldn't replace R13-15, nor follow heavy strength/oly work without a load drop. *Conf: low-moderate — CrossFit-specific dose-response data are sparse (evidence gap).*

## 5. Concurrent Training
**R17.** Interference effect is real, scales with endurance modality/frequency/duration: strength-only beats concurrent for strength (SMD1.76 vs 1.44), hypertrophy (1.23 vs 0.85), power (0.91 vs 0.55); running interferes more than cycling. *Conf: high.* — Wilson et al., J Strength Cond Res 2012;26(8):2293-2307 (pubmed 22002517).

**R18.** Within a session: strength before conditioning, not after — preserves lower-body dynamic strength (+6.9%) with no measured cost elsewhere. *Conf: moderate-high.* — Eddens/van Someren/Howatson, Sports Med 2018;48(1):177-88 (PMC5752732).

**R19.** ≥48h before repeating heavy loading of the same pattern: muscle protein synthesis peaks ~24-48h post-exercise, returns near baseline ~36-72h; repeated bouts <24h apart can blunt the synthetic response. *Conf: moderate-high (mechanistic marker).* — Damas et al., J Physiol 2016.

**R20.** Separate hard strength and hard conditioning by ≥6-8h same day, or different days; limit same-session heavy-strength+high-intensity-conditioning pairing to ≤2×/wk. *Conf: low-moderate — extrapolated from R17-19, not directly tested (evidence gap).*

## 6. Movement-Pattern Recovery
**R21.** Space heavy loading of the same pattern (hinge/squat/press/pull) ≥48h apart regardless of exercise label — pattern-level fatigue (shared prime movers/connective tissue/neural pathways) matters more than exercise novelty. *Conf: moderate, inferred from R2/R4/R19.*

**R22.** Plyometric volume: 20-80 contacts/session as a recreational floor; higher-performing specialist programs use >80 jumps/session, 3-4×/wk (>240-320/wk), 6-8wk for sprint/jump gains — scale down for non-specialists. *Conf: moderate (specialist populations).* — Sáez de Villarreal et al., J Strength Cond Res 2009 (pubmed 19197203).

**R23.** Plyometrics done fresh, early in session, low fatigue — same neural-precision/injury logic as R7-R8. *Conf: high mechanistic, low direct RCT isolating fatigued-vs-fresh injury rate.*

## 7. Session Structure
**R24.** Order: warm-up → activation → power (oly/plyo) → primary strength → accessory → conditioning. *Conf: high* (synthesis of R7, R18, ACSM sequencing principle).

**R25.** Session length: 45-75min (10 warm-up, 20-30 strength/power, 10-15 accessory, 10-20 conditioning). *Conf: low-moderate — practical envelope, not itself an RCT outcome.*

**R26.** 4-day split, mandatory heavy deadlift+push-press day: keep ≥48h from any other heavy hinge/heavy overhead-press session (R21); next day should be lower-intensity/rest/conditioning-only given the fatigue cost of heavy pull+press paired. Example: D1 heavy squat+oly pull+accessory; D2 conditioning-priority+upper accessory; D3 heavy deadlift+push press+core; D4 oly technique/power+press/pull strength+short conditioning finisher. *Conf: moderate — spacing logic well-evidenced; specific template is applied reasoning, not itself studied.*

## 8. Progression
**R27.** Double progression (fixed sets, rep-range e.g. 6-8; add reps to top of range both sets, then add load, reset to bottom) is a standard low-risk overload model. *Conf: moderate — widely used, few RCTs isolate it vs. linear progression.*

**R28.** Autoregulation (RPE/RIR, velocity-based) matches or modestly beats fixed %-based programming for strength, especially VBT; benefit less clear for already-periodized trained lifters. *Conf: moderate — mixed by training status.* — systematic reviews, PMC7810043; network meta-analysis, ScienceDirect S1728869X25000590.

**R29.** Deload cadence: ~every 4-8wk of high volume/intensity, one reduced-load week (~40-60% normal volume). Direct evidence thin: one RCT found a 1-wk mid-program deload modestly reduced lower-body strength gains with no effect on hypertrophy/power vs. continuous training. *Conf: low — contested; program by monitored fatigue, not rigid calendar, where possible.* — Coleman et al., PeerJ 2024;12:e16777 (PMC10809978).

## Evidence Gaps
Direct CrossFit/WOD dose-response data (AMRAP, for-time, high-rep oly EMOMs) are almost nonexistent — R8, R16, R20, R26 extrapolate from adjacent literature. Rest-interval/hypertrophy findings are contested across 2016-2025 reviews. Deload efficacy lacks robust RCT support (one trial suggests possible small strength cost, not benefit). Movement-pattern-level (vs. exercise-level) recovery spacing is inferred, not directly tested.
